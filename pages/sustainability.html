AI Sustainability

My Research

My research into the sustainability of AI focused on large-scale and one-time CO2 emissions, as well as small-scale, continuously accumulated emissions, in order to draw contrast between the total and unseen environmental impacts of AI.

It took data from the training of one relatively simple AI model, especially when compared to newer versions, and demonstrated how it dwarfed emissions from known environmental challenges. It also compared an average ChatGPT conversation with the equivalent amount of everyday action to show how those choices accumulate over time to build a carbon footprint.

Personal Reflection

When I use AI, I tend to use it as a second opinion and sanity check. I like to have it read over my emails for clarity, check my code for bugs, or scan texts to make sure I've fully grasped their themes. Sometimes, I prompt it experimentally or with thoughts about my day to see what it says, but I never rely on it for all the answers, as I don't really trust it.

I know that my AI usage adds to my carbon footprint. With the statistic of 10 queries producing 43.2 grams of CO2, I likely am producing around that amount every day or two. This adds to the carbon footprint that I've developed through technology usage, driving, and other environmentally impactful choices I make.

I am willing to be more conscious about my use of AI and the kinds of prompts I create. I think I could do a better job of providing prompts that get to the point and are clear, rather than prompts that are more wordy and similar to how I would talk to a person. This would reduce the interpretive work AI has to do and give me more efficient answers to reduce the follow-up prompts I send.

However, I won't give up AI entirely. I know that AI's environmental impacts are a large result of training and data processing rather than my individual use. AI will not go away, especially in my career field of CS, and to give up my usage of it only disadvantages myself when I could use it as a tool to boost me forward. If everyone gives up AI, there may be a greater impact, but on an individual level, there is not much I can directly do environmentally.

Systemic vs. Individual Change

A single individual deciding to no longer use AI will not put a dent into its carbon footprint. The industry will continue to make demands for water, power, and raw materials to supplement its millions of other users. However, individuals can speak out to inspire others and create real change.

Part of the systemic issue with AI is that tech companies are able to abstract how AI works. Instead of users picturing it as a process of collecting data, analyzing prompts, and running on real hardware, they see it as a "cloud" that simply listens and responds. This is not the user's fault. It is a perception designed by tech companies that hides the environmental demand and responsibility of AI.

That perception is enforced by quick responses, loading screens like three dots to give off the impression that the AI is "thinking," and the general belief of the Internet existing somewhere online rather than on hardware. This issue can only be solved on the systemic level by tech companies. The only way that will happen is if individual users speak out and draw attention to the issue, inspiring large numbers of people to either stop or at least become more conscious of how they use AI.

In addition, users can demand change such as companies using renewable energy and being sustainable about how they gather their resources. These environmental choices are a lot more important to the overall sustainability of AI, and require attention to reduce AI's impact.

Who Is Responsible?

I believe that everyone is responsible for this action, individuals and tech companies alike. AI users and tech companies both profit off the environment, often without addressing the harm they are doing.

Because tech companies are designed to profit, the choice falls to the user to demand change that only the companies can do effectively. With combined effort and systemic change, together they can take control of their choices and create a more sustainable world.
