Network Analysis

In my network, there are two nodes that are very central. My generated "User Data" is central because that is what creates prompts and responses for every AI tool. Without it, there is no data for the rest of the network and the companies within it to build off of. It shows the amount of control the user has over this process-while they cannot decide where their data goes, they have a lot of control over what they offer. In addition, the Gemini node is very central. Google was very transparent about how they share user data, and said they used third-party services for a lot of Gemini's data management, including human reviewers and safety regulations. This turned it into an important bridge connecting my data with third-party companies.

Clusters emerged between Anthropic and Google, as well as Microsoft and OpenAI. This is because these companies work together, sharing AI training and data storage platforms (Google Cloud and Microsoft Servers respectively), allowing them to benefit from each other's technology and share data control. This is likely why they are some of the most-used AI companies-they are able to use each other's power. In addition, there were clusters between the companies that display ads, and advertisers who use and provide them with data. This demonstrates that a lot of data is ultimately used for monetization, even if it is not directly sold to third-party companies when it is created.

I was surprised about how interconnected the AI companies are. I did not realize that they often share storage systems or even technology, like how Copilot uses ChatGPT's model as its base. I expected to see my data flow from the AI models I use to third-party services and major companies, but didn't know how accessible and closely related the models really are. This made me rethink how my data is distributed and who has access to it.

The network is missing a lot of the invisible labor that supports this data transfer. The data needs to be stored on hardware, sent across countries and to different centers, and managed by people to ensure privacy and security. None of this is visualized in this graph, making the viewer take that labor for granted. In addition, this graph is only shaped by information from the companies themselves, which may not be entirely accurate or worded in a way that creates confusion.

Artist Statement

My network reveals that while users sometimes feel they hold the power when talking to a chatbot, the real power lies with the companies behind the AI. As this network visualizes, the user mostly serves as an output of data, and acts as a resource for the companies rather than a customer. It also shows how the companies hold power because of the way they work with each other, such as sharing data processors and storage methods, represented by their overlapping groups and edges.

This presentation makes the companies and the kinds of services they use very visible. However, many of the companies conceal what exact third parties they outsource data to, causing questions about how safe our data really is. The labor they use in this outsourcing is invisible, and the way we perceive it is mostly controlled by what the company decides to tell us. A network structure with a stronger focus on the most popular third-party companies for data control, such as the most common security analyzers or training methods, would reveal more of that side of the story. It would better visualize the endpoints of our private data, and avoid the one-sided picture the AI companies are able to paint by limiting the details they give on where data is outsourced to.
