Critical Analysis with Algorithmic Tools

An analysis comparing Voyant Tools and ChatGPT as machine-reading systems.

Text Analyzed

Text: Young Americans are embracing "Chinamaxxing". That's a soft power boost for Beijing

Read the CNN article

Voyant Analysis

Voyant showed that the most used words were "Chinese", "China", and "Americans". The trends showed that Chinese was used the most at the beginning, then China in the middle, and Americans towards the end. The word cloud showed an interesting link between Chinese and Americans, and had links between American and feel as well as China and life, but otherwise many of the words did not seem correlated.

GPT Analysis

Prompt: "Interpret this text:" "What do you believe the most important takeaway from this article is?" "To you, is it a warning, a neutral observation, or subtle advocacy?" This last question was in response to GPT asking, "Now I'm curious - when you read it, did it feel like a warning, a neutral observation, or subtle advocacy?"

Comparison

Voyant presents different biases from ChatGPT. Voyant provides objective data and highlights existing patterns, allowing the user to make their own analysis. However, it limits interpretation to only words that the author used. On the other hand, ChatGPT interprets the text for the user. Even though it offers certain quotes and supporting evidence for its analysis, it still makes an argument, while Voyant does not.

Reflection

When machines read literature for us, we gain added insight into trends and context that we might not have seen reading on our own. Voyant showed how the article focused on different themes throughout the text, such as how the usage of different words changed from the beginning to the end. Seeing the most used words change from "Chinese" to "China" to "Americans" told me that the overarching theme of the article was likely about Chinese trends and background context, then China as a region and political power, then Americans and how they are affected. While this conclusion is influenced by my own reading of the article, Voyant provided me with evidence to support my own personal interpretation, which I may not have thought of otherwise. This demonstrates that Voyant sees meaning as patterns and word links, which is helpful for original interpretation but limits meaning to what is quantifiable.

ChatGPT had a similar influence on my interpretation, but it felt as if the bot took more control of what I should see. Given an open prompt like "interpret this," ChatGPT had free control to derive its own meaning from the article. It helped me quickly see overall trends in the text, and was able to provide me with background information that better helped me understand the context. However, what was lost was my own interpretation, as ChatGPT tried to convince me of the one it created by leaving out other perspectives and making its own feel definitive. In addition, some of the context that ChatGPT offered may have been oversimplified to avoid too much nuance in its answer. It provided a simple, one-line definition of "soft power", a complicated topic that is central to the article's theme. This showed that GPT sees "meaning" as an interpretation of the article, rather than the quantitative data of word patterns.

Attribution and AI Use

  • Tools: Voyant Tools; ChatGPT
